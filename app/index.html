How machines are built

Every robot is a bit like a person. It senses, it thinks, and then it acts.

SensorsEyes, ears and skin. They take information in.
MicrocontrollerThe brain. It decides what to do.
Motors, lights, buzzerHands, face and voice. They do the action.
BatteryFood. It gives the energy.
WiresNerves. They connect everything.

Electronics treasure hunt

Walk around the house together. For each thing, tap what it has inside. Stuck? Tap “Show hints” and a yellow dot marks the right answers.

For parents

Short, hands-on sessions of 20–30 minutes work best at age 7. Show the real part, use the app to explain it, then find it in the house.

Safety rule: say it every time

  • We learn with batteries only.
  • Never touch wall sockets, open chargers, or open appliances that are plugged in.
  • Always ask an adult before opening anything.

Learning path

  1. Age 7–8: circuits, basic parts, ScratchJr, unplugged coding games, “computer = input, process, output”.
  2. Age 8–10: Scratch, micro:bit, LEDs and motors, beginner robots (Botley, LEGO SPIKE), logic-gate games.
  3. Age 10–13: Python, Arduino or Raspberry Pi robots, binary and simple logic circuits.
  4. Teens: quantum computing basics. Build strong maths now; it is the real foundation.

What to buy first

  • Snap Circuits Jr. (SC-100): most Level 1–2 parts, with circuit symbols printed on them
  • ScratchJr app (free)
  • Hello Ruby: Journey Inside the Computer (book)
  • Later: micro:bit and a small robot kit

How to teach with this app

  • One or two parts per session. Hold the real part while reading its card.
  • Tap Read to me, then ask the Think! question before showing the answer.
  • Praise debugging: “You found the bug!” builds a problem-solver.
  • Let him teach the card back to you.
