# Maths Course Guide for Parents

Ollie the Octopus guides two adventures: **Numbers** and **Mathematics**, from counting apples (1st standard) to algebra, powers and Pythagoras (10th standard). Topics for 11th–12th standard are planned for later.

- Every step asks about 6–12 questions, generated fresh each time, so replaying gives new practice.
- A wrong answer gets a hint first; after a second try the answer is shown and the child moves on.
- Each step ends with a score (up to 3 stars for answers right first time).
- **Younger children** unlock steps one at a time; **older children** can open later parts straight away (each part says from which class).
- **Wrong choices come from real mistakes**, so a wrong answer tells you something. For example, "the value of the 7 in 730" offers 7, 70, 700 and 7000 (mixing up places), and number names include the swapped-digit mix-up (95 and 59).
- **Questions grow with the class.** For example, column sums use 3-digit numbers from Class 4; fractions add "1/4 of 20" from Class 4 and equivalent fractions ("2/3 = ?/9") from Class 5; area adds "find the missing side" from Class 6.

## 🔟 Ollie's number adventure
Count, compare and understand big numbers.

### Part A · Counting
_From 1st standard. Count objects, number names, before and after, more and less._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 🖐️ **Count to 10** | How many can you see? | Counting one by one tells us how many. | Count spoons, steps and shoes around the house. |
| 2. 🔟 **Count to 20** | Bigger groups. | Counting in groups of 5 or 10 is faster. | Count coins together, making piles of 5. |
| 3. 🔤 **Number names** | 7 is seven. | Numbers can be written in figures (7) and words (seven). | Write cheques or shopping lists together with numbers in words. |
| 4. ↔️ **Before, after, between** | What comes next? | After means one more, before means one less. | On a calendar, ask which date comes after today. |
| 5. ⚖️ **More or less** | > < = | > means more than, < means less than, = means the same. | Compare prices in a shop: which is more? |

### Part B · Place value
_After Part A · open from 3rd standard. Tens, ones and hundreds; skip counting; odd and even._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6. 🧊 **Tens and ones** | Rods and cubes. | A two-digit number is made of tens and ones: 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones. | Bundle sticks or straws in tens with rubber bands. |
| 7. 💯 **Hundreds, tens and ones** | Three-digit numbers. | In 352 the 3 means three hundreds, the 5 means five tens, the 2 means two ones. | Use ₹100, ₹10 and ₹1 coins (or paper notes) to make numbers. |
| 8. 🦘 **Skip counting** | 2, 4, 6, 8… | Skip counting is the first step to times tables. | Count shoes in pairs: 2, 4, 6… |
| 9. 🧦 **Odd and even** | Can it be shared in pairs? | Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8. Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9. | Pair up socks: an odd number leaves one without a partner. |

### Part C · Big numbers
_After Part B · open from 5th standard. Lakhs and crores, rounding and negative numbers._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10. 🏦 **Lakhs and crores** | Indian place value. | 1 lakh = 1,00,000 and 1 crore = 1,00,00,000. | Read house prices or cricket crowd numbers from the newspaper together. |
| 11. 🎯 **Rounding** | Nearest 10, 100, 1000. | Look at the digit to the right: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less rounds down. | Round shopping prices to estimate the total bill. |
| 12. 🌡️ **Negative numbers** | Below zero. | On a number line, numbers get bigger to the right. −2 is bigger than −5. | Look at temperatures of cold places like Leh in winter. |

## ➕ Ollie's maths adventure
From adding apples to algebra and Pythagoras.

### Part A · Add and subtract
_From 1st standard. With objects first, then two-digit sums with carrying._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. ➕ **Adding within 10** | Put groups together. | Adding puts groups together to find the total. | Add fruits in the basket: 3 bananas and 2 apples. |
| 2. ➖ **Taking away within 10** | How many are left? | Taking away tells us how many are left. | Eat 2 of 7 grapes and ask how many are left. |
| 3. 🧮 **Adding bigger numbers** | Ones first, then tens. | Column addition: ones first, carry to the tens. | Add two bus or train numbers you see on the way. |
| 4. 🧮 **Subtracting bigger numbers** | Borrow when you need to. | Column subtraction: ones first, borrow from the tens when needed. | Work out change from ₹100 when buying something. |

### Part B · Times and share
_After Part A · open from 3rd standard. Multiplication, tables, division and money problems._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5. 🧺 **Groups of** | Multiplication is repeated adding. | 3 groups of 4 is 3 × 4 = 12. | Arrange sweets in equal rows and count them by rows. |
| 6. ✖️ **Times tables** | 2 to 12. | Knowing tables by heart makes bigger sums easy. | Practise one table a week in the car or at dinner. |
| 7. 🍬 **Sharing equally** | Division. | 12 ÷ 3 = 4 because 3 × 4 = 12. | Share snacks equally between family members. |
| 8. 💰 **Money problems** | Rupees and change. | Word problems: find the numbers, decide add/subtract/multiply/divide, then solve. | Let your child pay at a shop and check the change. |

### Part C · Parts and shapes
_After Part B · open from 5th standard. Fractions, decimals, percentages, area and perimeter._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9. 🍕 **Fractions** | Parts of a whole. | In 3/8, the whole is cut into 8 equal parts and 3 are taken. | Cut a roti or pizza into equal parts and name the fractions. |
| 10. 🔸 **Decimals** | ₹12.50 and 3.75 kg. | Line up the decimal points when adding or subtracting. | Add prices on a restaurant bill together. |
| 11. 💯 **Percentages** | Discounts and marks. | To find x% of a number, multiply by x and divide by 100. | Work out the sale price during a festival sale. |
| 12. 📐 **Area and perimeter** | Inside and around. | Rectangle: perimeter = 2 × (l + w), area = l × w. | Measure a room or a book and find its area and perimeter. |

### Part D · Algebra and beyond
_After Part C · open from 7th standard. Integers, equations, powers and Pythagoras. 11th–12th topics are coming later._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13. 🌡️ **Integers** | Adding negatives. | −3 + 5 = 2, and 4 − (−2) = 6. | Use a lift: go up 5 floors from basement level −2. |
| 14. 🔎 **Solve for x** | Find the mystery number. | To solve 3x + 4 = 19: subtract 4, then divide by 3. x = 5. | Make up mystery-number riddles: I think of a number, double it and add 3… |
| 15. ⚡ **Powers and roots** | 2⁵ and √144. | a² × a = a³; √81 = 9 because 9 × 9 = 81. | Find the square numbers on a 100-square chart. |
| 16. 📏 **Pythagoras** | a² + b² = c². | Pythagoras: c² = a² + b², so c = √(a² + b²). | Check a 3-4-5 triangle with a measuring tape: it makes a perfect corner! |

## Question kinds in each step
| Step | Question kinds |
|---|---|
| Adding / taking away within 10 | Sums with pictures; from Class 2 also missing numbers (3 + ? = 7) |
| Adding / subtracting bigger numbers | Column sums with carrying and borrowing; 3-digit numbers from Class 4 |
| Hundreds, tens and ones | Read the blocks; value of a digit (wrong choices are the same digit in other places) |
| Times tables | Up to ×5 (Class 1–2), ×10 (Class 3), ×12 (Class 4 and up) |
| Money problems | Change, totals, sharing a bill, two things bought together |
| Negative numbers | Bigger or smaller, the smallest of four, and temperature changes in cold places (Leh, Gulmarg…) |
| Fractions | What fraction is shaded; which is bigger; a fraction of a number (Class 4+); equivalent fractions (Class 5+) |
| Decimals | Adding, comparing (3.2 or 3.15?), and change from ₹20/₹50/₹100 |
| Percentages | x% of an amount, sale prices, and test marks as a percentage |
| Area and perimeter | Area and perimeter of a rectangle; the missing side from the area (Class 6+) |
| Integers | Always with at least one negative number: −3 + 5, 4 − (−2) |
