# Language Course Guide for Parents

Polly the Parrot guides three adventures: **Alphabets**, **Words** and **Sentences**. Polly reads every question aloud in your child's chosen voice.

- Every step asks about 6–12 questions, generated fresh each time, so replaying gives new practice.
- A wrong answer gets a hint first; after a second try the answer is shown and the child moves on.
- Each step ends with a score (up to 3 stars for answers right first time).
- **Younger children** unlock steps one at a time; **older children** can open later parts straight away (each part says from which class).
- **Questions match the class.** Words and sentences come from three levels, like Chip's computer path: **Class 1–3**, **Class 4–7** and **Class 8–12**. Most questions are at the child's level, with about one in four from the level below for review. The word and sentence banks are in `web/src/content/practice/languageData.js`.
- **Wrong choices are real mistakes** (one goose, two "gooses"; "becuase"; "Their going to school"), so a wrong answer shows what to practise. Every question has exactly one right answer.

## 🔠 Polly's alphabet adventure
Meet every letter, its sound and its order.

### Part A · Letters and sounds
_From 1st standard. Meet all 26 letters, their sounds, and big and small letters._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 🅰️ **Letters A to M** | Tap each letter to hear it. | The first 13 letters, each with a word that starts with it. | Point to things at home that start with each letter: A for almirah, B for bucket… |
| 2. 🔤 **Letters N to Z** | The rest of the alphabet. | The last 13 letters of the alphabet. | Sing the ABC song together and point at each letter. |
| 3. 👂 **Starting sounds** | Which letter does it start with? | Every word starts with a sound, and each sound has a letter. | Play “I spy with my little eye, something beginning with…”. |
| 4. 🔠 **Big and small letters** | Match A with a. | Capital letters and small letters are the same letter in two shapes. | Look at a newspaper headline and find capital letters, then small ones. |
| 5. ➡️ **What comes next?** | Find the missing letter. | The alphabet always goes in the same order. | Cover one letter on an alphabet chart and ask which one is hidden. |

### Part B · Letter skills
_After Part A · open from 3rd standard. Vowels, alphabetical order and finding letters in words._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6. 🗣️ **Vowels and consonants** | A, E, I, O, U are special. | A, E, I, O and U are vowels. Every word needs at least one vowel sound. | Pick a word from a storybook and count its vowels together. |
| 7. 🔢 **Put letters in order** | Tap them in ABC order. | Alphabetical order is how dictionaries and class lists are arranged. | Put your family's names in alphabetical order together. |
| 8. 🔍 **Letter hunt** | Which word starts with it? | Finding the first letter helps with reading and spelling. | On a walk, spot shop signs and read their first letters. |
| 9. 📚 **Words in ABC order** | Order words like a dictionary. | Words can be put in ABC order by their first letter, just like in a dictionary. | Open a dictionary together and find a word by its first letter. |

## 📖 Polly's word adventure
Read, build and understand words.

### Part A · First words
_From 1st standard. Read short words, build them, rhymes and opposites._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 🖼️ **Picture words** | Read the word for the picture. | Short words like cat and sun can be read by sounding out each letter. | Label things at home with sticky notes: door, fan, bed. |
| 2. 🧩 **Build the word** | Tap letters to spell it. | Words are built from sounds, one letter at a time. | Use magnetic letters or paper squares to build words together. |
| 3. 🎵 **Rhyming words** | Words that sound alike. | Rhyming words end with the same sound. | Make up silly rhymes together: “A frog on a log”. |
| 4. ↔️ **Opposites** | Hot and cold, big and small. | Opposites are pairs of words with very different meanings. | Play the opposite game: you say “up”, your child says “down”. |

### Part B · Word power
_After Part A · open from 3rd standard. Plurals, spelling, kinds of words and similar words._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5. 👯 **One or many** | cat → cats, child → children. | Plurals usually add s or es, but some words change completely (child → children). | Count things at home: one spoon, two spoons; one knife, two knives. |
| 6. ✍️ **Spell it right** | Pick the correct spelling. | Tricky words are worth learning by heart. | Keep a “tricky words” list on the fridge and practise one a day. |
| 7. 🏷️ **Kinds of words** | Naming, doing, describing. | Nouns name, verbs do, adjectives describe. | Pick a sentence from a book and find one noun, one verb and one adjective. |
| 8. 🟰 **Same meaning** | Big and large mean the same. | Synonyms are words with the same or similar meaning. | Ask for another word for “nice” or “good” when your child writes. |

### Part C · Word builder
_After Part B · open from 6th standard. Prefixes, suffixes and words that sound the same._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9. 🧱 **Prefixes and suffixes** | un-, re-, -ful, -less. | un- and im- mean not, re- means again, -ful means full of, -less means without. | Find words with un-, re-, -ful and -less in a newspaper. |
| 10. 👂 **Sound-alike words** | there, their, they're. | Homophones sound alike but have different meanings and spellings. | Write a silly note with a homophone mistake and let your child fix it. |

## 💬 Polly's sentence adventure
Build sentences and become a grammar detective.

### Part A · Making sentences
_From 1st standard. Word order, capital letters, full stops and question marks._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 🧱 **Build a sentence** | Put the words in order. | Words must be in the right order to make sense. | Cut a sentence into word cards and let your child rebuild it. |
| 2. 🔠 **Capital and full stop** | Which one is written correctly? | Sentences start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. | Check your child's writing together for capitals and full stops. |
| 3. ❓ **Asking or telling?** | ? or . or ! | Questions end with ?, statements with ., and strong feelings with !. | Take turns: one asks a question, the other answers with a telling sentence. |
| 4. 🕳️ **Fill the gap** | Which word makes sense? | Reading the whole sentence helps you find the missing word. | Read a story and pause before a word. Let your child guess it. |

### Part B · Better sentences
_After Part A · open from 4th standard. Joining words, tenses and punctuation._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5. 🔗 **Joining words** | and, but, because, so, or. | and adds, but contrasts, because gives a reason, so gives a result, or gives a choice. | Finish sentences together: “I am happy because…”. |
| 6. ⏳ **Yesterday, today, tomorrow** | Past, present and future. | Tense tells us when something happens. | Talk about what you did yesterday and what you will do tomorrow. |
| 7. ✳️ **Punctuation** | Commas, ! and ? | Commas separate lists and names; ! shows strong feeling; ? asks. | Read a sentence aloud without pauses, then with them. Hear the difference! |

### Part C · Grammar detective
_After Part B · open from 6th standard. Subject and predicate, active and passive, reported speech._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8. 🎯 **Subject and predicate** | Who or what is it about? | Every sentence has a subject and a predicate. | In a news headline, find who it's about (subject) and what happened (predicate). |
| 9. 🔄 **Active and passive** | Ravi wrote → was written by Ravi. | Passive voice: object + was/is/will be + past participle + by + doer. | Spot passive sentences in a science textbook: “Water is boiled…”. |
| 10. 💬 **Reported speech** | Direct to indirect. | In reported speech, the tense usually goes back and pronouns change. | Retell what a friend said at school, starting with “She said that…”. |

## What changes with the class
| Step | Class 1–3 | Class 4–7 | Class 8–12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Words in ABC order | By the first letter (ball, fish, kite) | Same first letter: look at the second (bat, bed, bin) | Look further (plan, plane, plant) |
| Picture words / Build the word | cat, bus, gem | rabbit, candle, turtle | telescope, pineapple, hourglass |
| Rhyming words | cat–hat, moon–spoon | Rhymes spelled differently: four–floor, said–bread | (Class 4–7 words) |
| Opposites | hot–cold, near–far | borrow–lend, arrive–depart | scarce–plentiful, ascend–descend |
| One or many | cat–cats, box–boxes | baby–babies, leaf–leaves, child–children | crisis–crises, bacterium–bacteria, sister-in-law–sisters-in-law |
| Spell it right | said, they, because | Wednesday, separate, neighbour | accommodate, rhythm, questionnaire |
| Kinds of words | noun, verb, adjective (one word) | + adverb, in a sentence | + pronoun and preposition; the same word in different jobs ("water the plants" / "drink water") |
| Same meaning | big–large, end–finish | huge–enormous, fix–repair | abundant–plentiful, conceal–hide |
| Prefixes and suffixes | — | un-, re-, -ful, -less, -er | mis-, over-, under-, pre-, inter-, ir-, il-, -able, -hood |
| Sound-alike words | — | there/their/they're, to/too/two, tale/tail | principal/principle, stationary/stationery, weather/whether |
| Build a sentence | 4–6 words | 7–10 words | Longer sentences with although, if, which… |
| Capital and full stop | Capital at the start, full stop at the end | Names of people, places, days and months | Historical names and dates |
| Fill the gap | Everyday words | Pilot, telescope, tadpole | Verdict, erupt, circumference |
| Joining words | and, but, because, so, or | (same) | although, unless, until, while |
| Yesterday, today, tomorrow | Past, present, future | have finished, was watching, had left | If I had studied…, I wish I knew, will have been playing |
| Punctuation | Commas in lists, ? and ! | Speech marks, apostrophes (Riya's) | children's / girls', commas around extra information |
| Subject and predicate | Find the subject | (same) | Also find the predicate |
| Active and passive / Reported speech | — | Simple past, present and future | Continuous, perfect and questions; "The earth goes round the sun" stays in the present |
