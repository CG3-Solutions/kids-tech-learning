# Circuits & Gates: Course Guide for Parents

Volt the battery guides your child from a simple loop to the gates inside a computer. The course has **15 steps in three parts**, plus a **Free workshop** for building anything.

## How the course is organized
| Part | For | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Part A · Circuit basics** | Everyone, from 2nd standard | Loops, switches, conductors and parts. |
| **Part B · Switches that think** | After Part A · open from 4th standard | Wiring switches together makes decisions. These are logic gates. Younger children play the AND and OR puzzles. |
| **Part C · Thinking machines** | After Part B · open from 6th standard | Gates that add numbers and remember, like a real computer. |
| **Free workshop** | After step 4 · open from 4th standard | Build any circuit you like, with parts or with gates. |

- **Younger children** unlock steps one at a time, in order.
- **Older children** can open their part straight away (Part B from 4th standard, Part C from 6th). Part A basics open straight away from 5th.
- In "Meet the gates" and "Gate detective", children below 4th standard use only **AND** and **OR**. From 4th they also use **NOT** and **XOR**.
- Every step ends with a short question. Each step page has a **For parents** note, the same as below.

**Safety:** we learn with batteries only. Never touch wall sockets, open chargers, or plugged-in appliances.

## Part A · Circuit basics
_Everyone, from 2nd standard. Loops, switches, conductors and parts._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 🔄 **Close the loop** | Switch ON, the bulb glows. | Electricity only flows around a complete loop. A switch opens and closes the loop. | Flip a light switch together. Ask: where is the loop? (From the power, through the switch, through the bulb, and back.) |
| 2. 🔍 **Loop detective** | Find the broken wire. | If there is a gap anywhere in the loop, nothing works. Fixing the gap fixes the circuit. | When a toy stops working, check the batteries and loose wires together: you're being loop detectives. |
| 3. 🥄 **Will it light?** | Test spoons, coins, paper… | Metals let electricity through (conductors). Plastic, rubber, paper and wood stop it (insulators). | Look at a charger cable: metal inside to carry electricity, plastic outside to keep hands safe. |
| 4. 🔁 **Swap the part** | Bulb, motor, buzzer, LED. | The same loop can power light, movement or sound. An LED only works one way round. | Find a motor (fan), a buzzer (microwave beep) and an LED (TV standby light) at home. |
| 5. 🎚️ **Brighter or dimmer** | More batteries, or a knob. | More batteries push more electricity. A resistor knob holds some back, like a fan regulator. | Turn the fan regulator step by step together and watch the speed change. |

## Part B · Switches that think
_After Part A · open from 4th standard. Wiring switches together makes decisions. These are logic gates. Younger children play the AND and OR puzzles._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6. 🔒 **Both switches: AND** | Lid locked AND button pressed. | Two switches in a row: the loop works only when BOTH are ON. This is called AND. | A mixer grinder only runs when the lid is locked AND the switch is on. Find other AND machines (a lift: door closed AND button pressed). |
| 7. 🚪 **Either switch: OR** | Any car door turns on the light. | Two switches side by side: the loop works when EITHER one is ON. This is called OR. | In a car, opening any door turns on the inside light. A house with two doorbell buttons is another OR. |
| 8. 🌙 **The upside-down switch: NOT** | Light ON when it's NOT day. | A NOT switch does the opposite: pressed means OFF, not pressed means ON. | Street lights switch ON when it is NOT daytime. The fridge light is ON when the door is NOT closed. |
| 9. 🪜 **Staircase switch: XOR** | Two switches, one light. | With a staircase switch, flipping EITHER switch changes the light. This is called XOR. | Find a two-way switch at home (stairs or a long corridor) and try it from both ends. |
| 10. 🧲 **Electric fingers** | A switch pressed by electricity. | A relay or transistor is a switch pressed by electricity instead of a finger. Chips have billions of transistors. | Listen for the click of a relay when an inverter or a washing machine switches on. |
| 11. 🚦 **Meet the gates** | AND, OR, NOT, XOR symbols. | Engineers draw switch circuits as gate symbols. Each gate has a truth table: what it does for every input. | Ask your child to explain AND and OR with a real example. Truth tables are just “what happens if…” lists. |
| 12. 🕵️ **Gate detective** | Pick the right gate. | Real machines use gates to decide: seatbelt alarms, lifts, mixers and doorbells. | Invent a rule together (“the fan runs if it is hot AND someone is in the room”) and name the gate. |

## Part C · Thinking machines
_After Part B · open from 6th standard. Gates that add numbers and remember, like a real computer._

| Step | What your child does | What they learn | Talk about it at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13. ➕ **Adding machine** | Gates that add 1 + 1. | An XOR gate and an AND gate together add two bits. 1 + 1 = 10 in binary (that's two). | Connect this to Binary Magic: every sum a computer does is built from tiny adders like this one. |
| 14. 🧮 **Bigger adder** | Add two 4-bit numbers. | Chaining adders lets a computer add big numbers. The carry passes from one adder to the next. | Do a column addition on paper with carrying. The adder does exactly the same, in binary. |
| 15. 💾 **A circuit that remembers** | Set, reset, remember. | Two gates feeding each other can remember ON or OFF. This is how memory (RAM) stores bits. | Ask: why does a computer forget things in RAM when the power goes off? (The loop that remembers needs electricity.) |

## The big idea
A switch is ON or OFF, just like a bit (1 or 0) in **Binary Magic**. Wire switches together cleverly and they make decisions (gates). Gates can add numbers (the adder) and remember them (the latch). A computer is billions of tiny switches wired this way.
