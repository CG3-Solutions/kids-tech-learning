# Electronic Components for Kids, with Real-Life Examples

**One big idea runs through everything: electricity is like water flowing through pipes.**

For each part you'll find:
- **What is it?** A simple explanation to read aloud.
- **It's like…** An everyday comparison.
- **Find it around you.** Real places at home, on the road or at school.
- **Engineers draw it like this.** How its circuit symbol looks.
- **Think!** A question to ask him, with the answer.
- **Try it.** A small activity.

> The same content is in the **Spark Lab** app (`app/index.html`), with pictures, circuit symbols and a Read-to-me button.

## Big ideas
_Start here_

### ⚡ Electricity

**What is it?** Electricity is invisible energy that flows through wires, like water flowing through a pipe. We can’t see it, but we can see what it does: lights glow, fans spin and the TV plays.

**It's like…** Water flowing through a pipe.

**Find it around you:**
- Tube light glows when switched on
- Power cut: fans and lights stop
- Lightning is natural electricity

**Engineers draw it like this:** Electricity always needs a full loop to travel around.

**Think!** What stops working at home when there is a power cut?  
**Answer:** The fan, TV, fridge, lights and even the Wi-Fi.

**Try it:** During the next power cut, make a list together of everything that stopped.

### 🔄 Circuit

**What is it?** Electricity only flows when there is a complete round path. We call that path a circuit. If the path breaks anywhere, everything stops.

**It's like…** A school bus route. If one bridge on the route is broken, the bus can’t finish the trip.

**Find it around you:**
- Some Diwali light strings go dark if one bulb breaks
- Toy train track with a missing piece

**Engineers draw it like this:** Engineers draw circuits as neat boxes of lines. Each part sits somewhere on the loop.

**Think!** What happens if a train track has a missing piece?  
**Answer:** The train stops. Electricity stops too when the loop is broken.

**Try it:** Open the “Build a circuit” tab and flip the switch to break and join the loop.

## Level 1: Starter parts
_In every beginner kit_

### 🔋 Battery

**What is it?** A battery is a box of stored electricity. It pushes electricity out of one end (+), around the circuit, and back into the other end (–). When the energy is used up, the battery is “dead”.

**It's like…** A water pump pushing water around the pipes.

**Find it around you:**
- TV remote
- Wall clock
- Mobile phone (rechargeable)
- Inverter / UPS for power cuts
- Car and bike batteries

**Engineers draw it like this:** The long line is +, the short fat line is –.

**Think!** Why does Papa charge his phone every night?  
**Answer:** The phone battery gets used up during the day. Charging fills it again, like refilling a water bottle.

**Try it:** Take the batteries out of the TV remote. Does it still work? Put them back the wrong way round. Does it work now?

### 〰️ Wire

**What is it?** Wires are roads for electricity. Inside is metal (usually copper) that electricity travels through. Outside is plastic, which stops electricity from touching us.

**It's like…** A road or a water pipe.

**Find it around you:**
- Phone charger cable
- Wires on electric poles
- Earphone wire

**Engineers draw it like this:** A wire is just a straight line.

**Think!** Why is the charger wire covered with plastic?  
**Answer:** Plastic does not let electricity pass, so it keeps our hands safe. That’s why electricians wear rubber gloves too.

**Try it:** Look at an old, cut, unplugged cable with an adult. Find the shiny copper inside the plastic. _(Do this with an adult.)_

### 🔘 Switch

**What is it?** A switch opens or closes the path. ON means the path is joined and electricity flows. OFF means the path is broken and it stops.

**It's like…** A drawbridge. Down, the cars can cross. Up, the road is broken.

**Find it around you:**
- Light and fan switches
- Power button on TV and phone
- The fridge door button that turns the light on
- Torch button

**Engineers draw it like this:** A little gate that lifts up to break the line.

**Think!** How does the fridge light know when the door is open?  
**Answer:** There is a small switch near the door. The closed door presses it OFF. Open the door and it pops ON.

**Try it:** Open the fridge and find the little button. Press it with your finger and watch the light.

### 💡 Bulb

**What is it?** A bulb changes electricity into light. In an old-style bulb, electricity squeezes through a very thin wire that gets so hot it glows.

**It's like…** A stove coil that turns red when it gets hot.

**Find it around you:**
- Old yellow bulbs
- Tube lights
- Street lights
- Car headlights

**Engineers draw it like this:** A circle with a cross inside.

**Think!** Why does an old bulb become hot?  
**Answer:** Some of the electricity turns into heat instead of light.

**Try it:** Hold your hand near (not on) an old bulb and an LED bulb. Which one feels warmer? _(Do this with an adult.)_

### 🔔 Buzzer / speaker

**What is it?** Electricity makes a tiny plate shake very fast. The shaking makes sound.

**It's like…** Plucking a rubber band. It shakes and you hear it.

**Find it around you:**
- Doorbell
- Microwave and washing machine beeps
- Car reverse beep
- Alarm clock
- TV and phone speakers

**Engineers draw it like this:** Looks like a little bell cut in half.

**Think!** Put your hand on a speaker while music plays. What do you feel?  
**Answer:** Shaking! Sound is shaking air.

**Try it:** Play music on a speaker and put a few grains of rice on top. Watch them jump.

### ⚙️ Motor

**What is it?** A motor turns electricity into spinning or movement.

**It's like…** Your legs pedalling a cycle. Energy in, spinning out.

**Find it around you:**
- Ceiling and table fan
- Mixer grinder
- Washing machine drum
- Toy cars
- Water pump
- Phone vibration
- Lift

**Engineers draw it like this:** A circle with the letter M.

**Think!** How many motors can we find in our house?  
**Answer:** Often 10 or more! Fans, mixer, washing machine, pump, fridge, phones, toys…

**Try it:** Go on a motor hunt and count them. Write the number down and beat it next week.

## Level 2: Explorer parts
_Control the flow_

### 🚦 LED

**What is it?** An LED is a modern, tiny light. It uses very little electricity and stays cool. Electricity can go through it only one way, so if it’s connected backwards, it won’t glow.

**It's like…** A one-way street.

**Find it around you:**
- Red standby light on the TV
- Traffic signals
- White LED bulbs at home
- Phone torch
- Charging light: red then green
- Scrolling boards on buses

**Engineers draw it like this:** A triangle arrow with a bar, plus two arrows for the light coming out.

**Think!** Look closely at a traffic signal. What do you see?  
**Answer:** Lots of tiny dots. Each dot is an LED!

**Try it:** In “Build a circuit”, choose LED and turn it around. Does it still glow?

### 🟫 Resistor

**What is it?** A resistor slows down electricity. Too much electricity can burn small parts like LEDs, so a resistor keeps the flow safe.

**It's like…** A speed breaker near a school.

**Find it around you:**
- Iron box
- Toaster
- Geyser
- Room heater (they get hot on purpose)

**Engineers draw it like this:** A zig-zag line. Electricity has to wiggle through it.

**Think!** How does an iron box become hot?  
**Answer:** It has a special resistor inside. Slowing the electricity makes heat.

**Try it:** With Snap Circuits, light an LED with the resistor. Ask an adult why we never skip it.

### 🎚️ Knob (potentiometer)

**What is it?** A knob you turn to control how much electricity flows: more or less, faster or slower, louder or softer.

**It's like…** A water tap. Turn it more, more water comes.

**Find it around you:**
- Fan regulator
- Volume knob on radio
- Light dimmer

**Engineers draw it like this:** A zig-zag with an arrow pointing at it. The arrow is the part you turn.

**Think!** How do we make the fan go slower?  
**Answer:** Turn the regulator. It lets less electricity through.

**Try it:** Turn the fan regulator step by step and count how fast the blades go.

### 🫙 Capacitor

**What is it?** A capacitor stores a little electricity for a short time and then lets it all out quickly.

**It's like…** A small water tank that fills up and empties fast.

**Find it around you:**
- Camera flash
- Ceiling fan (helps it start)
- Charger light that fades after unplugging

**Engineers draw it like this:** Two short lines facing each other, with a gap in between.

**Think!** Why does the charger light stay on for a second after unplugging?  
**Answer:** The capacitor inside still has a little stored electricity.

**Try it:** Watch a phone camera flash in a dark room. That big flash came from a capacitor.

### ↪️ Diode

**What is it?** A diode is a one-way door for electricity. It can go one way, but never back.

**It's like…** The metro turnstile gate. You can only walk through one way.

**Find it around you:**
- Inside every phone charger
- Inside TVs and computers

**Engineers draw it like this:** The triangle points the way electricity is allowed to go.

**Think!** What happens if you drive the wrong way on a one-way road?  
**Answer:** You’re stopped! The diode stops electricity going the wrong way.

**Try it:** An LED is a diode that glows. Try it backwards in the circuit tab.

### 🧲 Electromagnet

**What is it?** When electricity flows through coiled wire, it becomes a magnet. Switch it off and the magnet goes away.

**It's like…** A magic hand that grabs only when you say so.

**Find it around you:**
- Scrapyard cranes lifting cars
- Inside speakers and motors
- Maglev trains

**Engineers draw it like this:** Curly loops of wire, with a line for the iron core.

**Think!** How does the crane drop the car?  
**Answer:** It switches the electricity off, and the magnet stops pulling.

**Try it:** Wrap wire around an iron nail and connect a battery for a few seconds. Pick up paper pins! _(Do this with an adult.)_

## Level 3: Sensors
_How machines feel the world_

### ☀️ Light sensor

**What is it?** A light sensor tells a machine whether it’s bright or dark.

**It's like…** Our eyes noticing that it’s night.

**Find it around you:**
- Street lights switch on by themselves in the evening
- Phone screen gets brighter outdoors

**Engineers draw it like this:** A part in a circle, with arrows of light shining in.

**Think!** How do street lights know it’s evening?  
**Answer:** A light sensor sees it getting dark and switches them ON.

**Try it:** Cover the top of a phone with your hand and watch the screen dim.

### 🌡️ Temperature sensor

**What is it?** A temperature sensor feels hot or cold.

**It's like…** Our skin feeling a hot cup of tea.

**Find it around you:**
- AC stops cooling when the room is cold enough
- Fridge
- Thermometer for fever

**Engineers draw it like this:** A box with a slanted line through it.

**Think!** How does the AC know when to stop?  
**Answer:** Its temperature sensor feels that the room is cool enough.

**Try it:** Use a digital thermometer on a cold glass of water and a warm cup. Compare.

### 🎤 Microphone

**What is it?** A microphone turns sound into electricity so machines can “hear”.

**It's like…** Our ears.

**Find it around you:**
- Phone calls
- Voice assistants listening for their name
- Video calls

**Engineers draw it like this:** A circle with a flat line on one side.

**Think!** How can Grandma hear you on a phone call?  
**Answer:** The microphone turns your voice into electricity, and the other phone’s speaker turns it back into sound.

**Try it:** Record your voice on a phone and play it back.

### 🦇 Distance sensor

**What is it?** It shouts a sound too high for us to hear and listens for the echo. The quicker the echo, the closer the thing is.

**It's like…** A bat finding its way in the dark.

**Find it around you:**
- Car parking sensor that beeps faster near a wall
- Robot vacuum cleaners

**Engineers draw it like this:** Two round “eyes”: one speaks, one listens.

**Think!** Why does the car beep faster near the wall?  
**Answer:** The echo comes back quicker, so the sensor knows the wall is close.

**Try it:** Shout in an empty hall or a well and listen for your echo.

### 🚪 Motion sensor

**What is it?** A motion sensor notices when something moves near it.

**It's like…** A guard who looks up when someone walks by.

**Find it around you:**
- Automatic doors at malls
- Hand dryers
- Lights that switch on when you walk in

**Engineers draw it like this:** Sensors are usually drawn as a part with arrows coming in.

**Think!** How does the mall door know we are coming?  
**Answer:** A motion sensor above the door sees us moving.

**Try it:** Next time at a mall, find the little sensor box above the automatic door.

### 👆 Push button & touch

**What is it?** A push button is ON only while you keep pressing it. A touch sensor feels your finger without a click.

**It's like…** A doorbell.

**Find it around you:**
- Doorbell
- Lift buttons
- Phone touch screen
- Fingerprint unlock

**Engineers draw it like this:** A bar above the gap. Press it down and it joins the line.

**Think!** What is the difference between a switch and a doorbell button?  
**Answer:** A switch stays ON. A doorbell button is ON only while you press it.

**Try it:** Press the doorbell and let go. Then switch a light on and let go. What’s different?

### 💧 Water sensor

**What is it?** A water sensor notices when water reaches it.

**It's like…** Feeling your socks get wet in a puddle.

**Find it around you:**
- Water-tank alarm that beeps when the tank is full
- Washing machine checking water level

**Engineers draw it like this:** Drawn like other sensors: a part that changes with the world around it.

**Think!** How does the tank alarm know the tank is full?  
**Answer:** The water touches the sensor at the top and it tells the buzzer to beep.

**Try it:** Listen for the tank alarm next time the motor fills the overhead tank.

## Level 4: Computer brain
_Age 8 and up_

### 🔀 Transistor

**What is it?** A transistor is a tiny switch turned ON or OFF by a small electric signal instead of a finger. A phone chip has billions of them. ON means 1 and OFF means 0. Everything a computer does is 1s and 0s flicking very fast.

**It's like…** A stadium with billions of lights flicking on and off to make pictures, shrunk smaller than a fingernail.

**Find it around you:**
- Inside every phone, laptop, TV and calculator
- Inside washing machines and microwaves

**Engineers draw it like this:** A circle with three legs. The small signal goes in the side leg.

**Think!** How many transistors are in a phone chip?  
**Answer:** More than 10 billion!

**Try it:** Use 5 cards with 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 dots. Flip them up or down to make numbers. That’s binary: how transistors count.

### 🔁 Relay

**What is it?** A relay lets a small electric signal switch on something much bigger.

**It's like…** A small child pressing a button that opens a huge gate.

**Find it around you:**
- Inverters
- Car starters
- Automatic water-pump controllers

**Engineers draw it like this:** A coil on one side, a switch on the other, joined by a dotted line.

**Think!** How can a tiny chip switch on a big water pump?  
**Answer:** It uses a relay as its helper.

**Try it:** Listen for the “click” when an inverter switches on during a power cut. That’s a relay.

### 🧠 Microcontroller

**What is it?** A microcontroller is a very small computer that follows instructions we write. It reads sensors, thinks, and controls motors, lights and buzzers.

**It's like…** The robot’s brain.

**Find it around you:**
- Washing machine: “If water is full, start spinning”
- Microwave: “Heat for 2 minutes, then beep”
- Traffic signal timer
- Toy robots
- Arduino and micro:bit

**Engineers draw it like this:** A box with lots of little legs (pins).

**Think!** What instructions does the washing machine follow?  
**Answer:** Fill water, wash, drain, spin, then beep. Step by step, like a program.

**Try it:** Play “robot parent”: give an adult step-by-step instructions to walk to a toy.

### 🔢 7-segment display

**What is it?** Seven little light bars. Turning different ones on makes each number.

**It's like…** Building numbers with seven matchsticks.

**Find it around you:**
- Digital clock
- Microwave timer
- Washing machine time left
- Petrol pump numbers

**Engineers draw it like this:** Seven bars shaped like the number 8.

**Think!** Which number uses all seven bars?  
**Answer:** 8!

**Try it:** Make 0–9 with seven matchsticks or ice-cream sticks.
